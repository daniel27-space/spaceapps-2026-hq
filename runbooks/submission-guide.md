## What to submit — checklist

Fill these on the team's Project page on spaceappschallenge.org. The written guide does not give word or character limits for any field. The video suggests 50–200 words for the high-level summary only. Image uploads on the page: JPG, PNG, or GIF, up to 10 MB. The page does not accept video or document uploads; host those elsewhere and paste a public link.

- [ ] **Project Name.** "What is the name of your project?" (Submission Guide: "Project Page Form Field Instructions".)
- [ ] **High-Level Project Summary.** "What did you develop? How does it address the challenge? Why is it important?" (same section.) Suggestion in the video, not a written limit: "We suggest 50 to 200 words."
- [ ] **Link to Project Demo.** A short demo: either a slide deck of **at most 7 slides, including title and intro slides**, or a video of **30 seconds or less**. Host it off-site (YouTube, Google Drive, GitHub, OneDrive, Dropbox, or similar). The link must be publicly accessible: **no password, permission, or registration**. (Submission Guide: form field + "Tips for Creating a Project Demo"; Judging Guide: "Minimum requirements".) "Projects that exceed these limits will not be eligible for Global Judging." (Submission Guide: "Tips… Limitations".) All form text and linked materials must be in English. Exception: a demo video may be spoken in another language **only if it has English subtitles**. Slide decks must be in English. (Submission Guide: "Language Requirements"; Judging Guide: "Minimum requirements".)
- [ ] **Link to Final Project.** A publicly accessible link to the finished work (website, cloud host, or code repository). Same no-login rule. "If judges can't access your project, it can't be evaluated or judged." (Submission Guide: "Link to Final Project" and "Sharing Files…".)
- [ ] **Detailed Project Description.** Extra detail. The written guide suggests: what it does; how it works; benefits; what you hope to achieve; tools, languages, hardware, or software used. (Submission Guide: "Detailed Project Description".) The video also asks you to consider: intended impact; how the project is creative; and factors such as energy, accessibility, or cost.
- [ ] **Use of Super Intelligence (SI).** "Did you utilize any Super Intelligence (SI) tools and software in preparing your solution? If yes, how did you use them?" SI (machine learning, chatbots, image generation, large language models) is permitted "as a tool to accelerate innovation and creativity." The written guide also says: "SI should not be used to (come up with your idea of how to solve the challenge)." — official wording, parentheses included. Generated content may not use, contain, or modify NASA branding or design elements (logos, flags, mission identifiers). You must clearly indicate where and how SI was used:
  - Images and videos: visible watermark that they are SI-generated.
  - Audio: acknowledge SI generation in descriptive text and metadata.
  - Code and data: acknowledge SI generation in descriptive text and metadata.
  The SI form answer is "for data-collection purposes only, and your response will not be seen by the judges." Judges can still see watermarks, descriptive text, or metadata in the project, and "will evaluate originality, intent, and execution based on the SI-assisted elements." (Submission Guide: "Use of Super Intelligence (SI)".) The video adds that the field is a drop-down: "select each applicable option from the drop down menu. If you did not use these tools, select I did not use SI."
- [ ] **NASA Data.** "Provide specific details about what NASA data and resources you used." "**To be eligible for a Global Award, you must use at least one NASA data or resource.**" (Submission Guide: "NASA Data"; Video, NASA Data section.)
- [ ] **Space Agency Partner Data and Other References.** "List all of the data, resources, and tools used in your project," including code, text, and images even if open source or free. You must have permission for any copyrighted material. Using 2026 Space Agency Partner data is "highly encouraged," not required. (Submission Guide: "Space Agency Partner Data and Other References".) Auto-captions after this part of the video say "All links must be publicly acceptable. Please note that this section is optional." "Acceptable" is likely "accessible." Treat NASA Data as required for a Global Award; treat this references field as probably optional, but fill it anyway. Suggestion: include every dataset, library, image, and tool, plus the pre-event practice prototype disclosure (see Team plan).
- [ ] **Images on the Project page (optional).** JPG, PNG, or GIF, up to 10 MB, upload or paste. No video or document uploads on the page itself. (Submission Guide: "Sharing Files…".)
- [ ] **Two checkboxes, then Submit for Judging** (see steps below). Certificates appear only after a successful submit.

There is no separate "challenge" field in the Project form instructions. Eligibility still requires the team to "Respond to one of the official NASA Space Apps Challenges." (Judging Guide: "Minimum requirements".) Confirm the team is on **Lunar Landing Site Navigator** before submit.

## How to submit (step by step on the site)

Path and editing (Submission Guide: "Editing Your Project Submission Page"; Video, first half):

1. Log in at [spaceappschallenge.org](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/).
2. Click your profile icon at the top right, then **My Team**.
3. Open the **Project** tab. It appears only once the hackathon starts (Sat 14 Nov 09:00 local time).
4. Click the blue **Edit** button.
5. Turn the edit **Lock** to ON while you work, so teammates know you are editing. Another member can still remove the lock and overwrite unsaved changes, so coordinate. Suggestion: only Nich edits the live form after the Sun 11:00 handoff; keep a shared draft elsewhere.
6. Click blue **Save Progress** often (right-hand side of the page). You may save as many times as you like. Saved-but-not-submitted changes are visible only to the team.
7. Paste public links. Before submit, set each file or site to public, then test every link in a private/incognito window (and, suggestion, on a phone). If you created a new website, the guide recommends a [FortiGuard Web Filter Classification](https://www.fortiguard.com/faq/wfratingsubmit) so judges' filters do not block it. Category: pick the best match (education, personal website and blogs, reference, or web-based application). Company Name: write "N/A". This is **not required**. NASA cannot choose the category or control FortiGuard's decision. (Submission Guide: "Sharing Files…".)
8. When every required field is complete, in the verification / additional-information area:
   - Check the box to agree to the Terms and Conditions.
   - Check the box to agree to "Confirm All Work is Original".
   - Click **Submit for Judging** (the video says this button is at the top of the page). You cannot submit if required sections are incomplete.
9. A banner and confirmation message should appear at the top. The video says your page then becomes viewable to anyone who visits it, and your participant certificate becomes available.
10. You may edit and resubmit as many times as you like until the deadline. "**Only your last saved submission will be reviewed by the judges.**" (Submission Guide: "Submitting Your Project".) The video says "Only your last submission will be reviewed by the judges." Suggestion: after a last-minute edit, click **Submit for Judging** again, then screenshot the confirmation banner.

Exact checkbox wording from the Judging Guide, "Eligibility for Global Judging":

- "I have read and understand the project submission requirements as contained in the NASA Space Apps Project Submission Guide, and I agree to the Participant Terms and Conditions and Privacy Policy."
- "I confirm that the submitted project represents my team's original work and that all external resources including code, text and images (even if they are open source or freely available) used in the project are listed in the References field of the project submission form. In creating our solution, my team has not used any copyrighted materials (i.e., music, images, text, etc.) that we don't have permission to use."

### After submit — certificates

Anyone who is registered and whose team clicks **Submit for Judging** by the deadline gets a participant certificate. (Submission Guide: "Accessing Your Participant Certificate"; Judging Guide: "The Purpose of NASA Space Apps".)

1. Profile icon → **Profile**.
2. Under "Participant Information", find "2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge".
3. Click the yellow **Download Participant Certificate** button. Choose paper size **Letter** (the guide writes "Letter (8.5x11in / 215x297mm)"). PDF only.
4. The guide suggests downloading from a laptop or desktop, not a mobile device.
5. The name on the certificate is the Full Name in your Profile. You can change it even after submit (Account Information → Change next to Full Name). Follow the NASA Space Apps Values when entering a name.
6. Problems: email web@spaceappschallenge.org with your username and team name.

## Judging criteria and eligibility (what matters at submit)

Five criteria, each scored 1–5, at every judging level (Judging Guide: "Judging Criteria"; Video from 10:48):

1. **Impact** — significance of the problem and the reach or benefit of the proposed solution.
2. **Creativity** — originality, or a meaningful improvement on existing ideas.
3. **Validity** — scientific / technical / logical feasibility, completeness, usability.
4. **Relevance** — it "fully and directly addresses the challenge," and "meaningfully integrates NASA data, utilizing it as a core component of the proposed solution rather than a superficial addition."
5. **Presentation** — clear story of the challenge, the solution, and why it matters. "Judges evaluate the demo and full project to compute this score." A strong demo can raise the Presentation score, but "the majority of judges' evaluation will be based on your project submission." (Submission Guide: "Link to Project Demo"; Judging Guide: "Presentation".)

The Judging Guide asks teams not to neglect Presentation: spend time on the pitch and the story.

### Minimum requirements for Global Judging

From Judging Guide, "Eligibility for Global Judging," plus Submission Guide, "Submitting Your Project":

- Respond to one official 2026 challenge.
- Every teammate is registered, has chosen the **same** Local Event (Singapore), is **not waitlisted**, and appears on the team's **Members** tab. The Team Owner must be on that same Local Event too. Teams cannot add members after submission closes. Local Event teams: at least 1 person; maximum 6. (Universal Event minimum is 2; that is not our case.)
- Complete all required Project fields.
- Provide a valid demo (7-slide or 30-second limit, public link, English / English subtitles).
- English on the Project page and in linked materials (video exception above).
- Click **Submit for Judging** before Sun 15 Nov 23:59 SGT.
- Tick both agreement boxes.
- Demo and final project **may not include** images, video, audio, or other likenesses of anyone under 18. (Submission Guide: "Submitting Your Project," pointing at the [Terms & Conditions](https://www.spaceappschallenge.org/legal/).)
- For Global Judging, teams are reviewed under NASA's China funding restriction policy (no prohibited bilateral activity with China or a Chinese-owned entity, as defined in the Judging Guide and the Terms). NASA decides eligibility. See the Judging Guide paragraph and the Terms if this could apply.

A participation certificate needs a successful submit by the deadline; it does not require advancing in judging.

### After the hackathon (for context)

- Phase 1: Local Event judges (recruited by the Local Lead) use the same five criteria and pick **Global Nominees**, announced 3 Dec 2026. Local awards, if any, are separate and do **not** automatically send a team to Global Judging. Only official Global Nominees go forward. (Judging Guide: Phase 1; Video, judging section.)
- Phase 2: expert Global Judges pick **Global Finalists** and **Honorable Mentions**, announced 17 Dec 2026. Honorable Mentions do not go to Executive Judging and are not eligible for Global Awards.
- Phase 3: NASA and Space Agency Partner executives nominate Global Finalists for one of ten Global Awards, announced 26 Jan 2027, with a virtual Winner's Celebration in early 2027. Award names: Best Use of Science; Best Use of Data; Best Use of Technology; Galactic Impact; Best Mission Concept; Most Inspirational; Best Storytelling Award; Global Connection Award; Art & Technology Award; Local Impact Award. (Judging Guide: Phase 3.)

## Common pitfalls and disqualifiers

These will block judging, Global Judging, and/or a Global Award. Each is from the official guides or video, not a suggestion.

- Miss **Submit for Judging** (saving progress is not enough) or submit after Sun 15 Nov 23:59 SGT. No late edits or exceptions. (Submission Guide: "Submitting Your Project"; Video.)
- Demo longer than 30 seconds, or a deck longer than 7 slides including title and intro. Written guide: not eligible for **Global Judging**. Video: to be eligible for a **Global Award**, you must keep to the limit and keep the demo public. Follow the stricter written rule: stay inside the limit.
- Demo or final-project link needs a login, password, or permission, or is broken. Test incognito. (Submission Guide: "Sharing Files…" and form fields.)
- Required fields left blank; submit button will not work. (Submission Guide: "Submitting Your Project"; Video.)
- Project page or linked materials not in English (unless a demo video has English subtitles). Slide decks must be in English. (Submission Guide: "Language Requirements".)
- No NASA data or resource: not eligible for a **Global Award**. (Submission Guide: "NASA Data".) Relevance scoring also expects NASA data as a core part of the solution, not a bolt-on. (Judging Guide: "Relevance".)
- SI-generated images or videos without a visible watermark; SI audio, code, or data without an acknowledgement; SI content that uses or modifies NASA logos, flags, or mission identifiers. (Submission Guide: "Use of Super Intelligence (SI)".)
- Likeness (photo, video, audio, or other) of anyone under 18 in the demo or project. (Submission Guide: "Submitting Your Project".)
- Teammates waitlisted, on a different event, missing from the Members tab, or more than six people. Members cannot be added after close. (Judging Guide: "Minimum requirements".)
- Copyrighted material used without permission, or references left off the list you promised in the originality checkbox. (Judging Guide: checkbox text; Submission Guide: "Space Agency Partner Data…".)
- A new custom website blocked by a web filter. FortiGuard classification is recommended, not required. (Submission Guide: "Sharing Files…".)
- Two people editing at once and overwriting unsaved work. (Submission Guide: "How to Edit Your Project Page".)
